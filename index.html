How technology changes cybersecurity

Qualitative impact; not an assertion of measured risk reduction.

Agentic AI · risk can increase

  • Indirect prompt injection can manipulate an agent's interpretation of supplier content.
  • Excessive agency and broad tool permissions can turn a model error into a business action.
  • MCP servers, tool schemas and connectors add supply-chain and delegated-authority risk.
  • RAG/vector poisoning and weak retrieval permissions can distort decisions or expose data.
  • Unbounded tool calls may cause cost, capacity or availability issues.

Agentic AI · risk can reduce

  • Automated anomaly triage and monitoring can help prioritise investigation.
  • Grounded retrieval can improve consistency when the underlying sources are trusted and access-controlled.
  • Automated policy and compliance checks can identify missing controls or deviations.
  • Audit trails can improve traceability when agent, tool and approval events are recorded securely.
  • Automation can reduce manual re-keying, but can also scale an incorrect rule.

Cloud & integration · risk can increase

  • Misconfiguration or cloud control-plane compromise can expose resources and privileges.
  • Workload identities, service accounts and OAuth grants expand non-human access paths.
  • Weak API authorisation can propagate permissions into ERP and finance workflows.
  • Tenant context can fail in caches, queues, vector stores, sessions, logs or asynchronous jobs.
  • Provider outages and cross-border processing create continuity and data-governance dependencies.

Cloud & integration · risk can reduce

  • Managed infrastructure and PaaS can reduce the burden of operating underlying components.
  • Centralised identity, policy and logging can improve consistency and visibility.
  • Managed key services, encryption and network controls can strengthen protection when correctly configured.
  • Cloud backup, replication and recovery services can improve resilience when restoration is tested.
  • Standardised APIs can improve control consistency when each operation is independently authorised.

Escalation and reporting

Proposed escalation examples based on potential business consequence.

SEV-1 · Critical

Material compromise

Confirmed or strongly suspected privileged cloud compromise, widespread customer-data exposure, compromised production release, major ransomware or recovery failure.

Immediate security and executive escalation; legal/privacy and customer notification assessed separately.
SEV-2 · High

Contained high-impact event

Unauthorised sensitive-system access, material API compromise, suspected malicious agent activity or significant supplier incident.

Escalate to CISO, relevant technology owner and affected business executive.
SEV-3 / 4

Moderate / low

Contained policy violations, suspicious but unconfirmed events, routine exceptions and unsuccessful attempts.

Record, investigate and manage through operational risk channels.

Implementation roadmap

Proposed horizons from the report, subject to baseline, dependency and capacity review.

Phase 1 · 0–3 months

Establish visibility

Assign risk owners; review privileged identities; inventory AI agents, MCP tools and critical suppliers; map customer-data flows; review backup and incident-response arrangements.

Phase 2 · 3–6 months

Strengthen controls

Improve identity/API controls; introduce AI-agent governance; strengthen software dependency assurance; improve tenant-aware access and monitoring.

Phase 3 · 6–12 months

Validate & optimise

Conduct AI/API security testing; verify software release integrity; test tenant isolation; run incident exercises and recovery validation; reassess residual risk.

Management dashboard: Track privileged-access coverage, software dependency assurance, agent-permission reviews, tenant-isolation testing, backup restoration, time to detect/contain and recovery against customer-approved RTO/RPO. Establish baselines before setting numeric targets.
